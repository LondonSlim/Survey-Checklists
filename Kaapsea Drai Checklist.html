Recommendations
Overplate the foot of the port bow with continuously welded 6.0mm mild steel (doubler 250mm x 700mm), aft end terminating aft of the forward bulkhead.
Overplate the starboard counter bottom, including the uxter plate, with continuously welded 6.0mm mild steel, from centreline to outboard edge and from the transom to the forward end of the uxter.
Overplate the four areas of deep pitting on the bottom in 5.0mm mild steel (see Appendix C).
Ring weld the eighty rivet points marked with chalk on the bottom (alternatively, overplate the bottom in full).
Black the hull above and below the waterline, including the counter bottom plate and weed hatch tunnel; black the rudder at the same time.
Cut an access hatch in the cabin sole forward of the engine compartment bulkhead so the bilge beneath the accommodation can be monitored.
Fit float switches to the bilge pumps so they operate automatically.
Mechanically cap the open gate valve to starboard of the engine room; when the vessel is next ashore, blank it externally with a continuously welded plate.
Make good the seal where the exhaust pipe passes through the shell plating.
Double-clamp each end of the below-waterline hose on the engine cooling water intake.
Fit an air filter to the engine air intake.
Secure the outboard tie rod retaining the fuel tank.
Ensure all fuel lines are made from copper pipe or hose specified to ISO 7840, and that the main shut-off valve is closed when the vessel is moored.
Seal the open spigot on top of the fuel tank and blank the gauge off-take.
Blank the two shut-off valves on the stubs below the fuel tank.
Replace the cracked fuel delivery hose and breather hose.
Create ready access to the stern gland.
Grease and test the anchor windlass before the vessel is used for navigation under her own power.
Carry out a thorough examination of the lifting equipment (bow crane) before use.
Replace the navigation lights.
Make good the loose cables and bare wires serving the side lights.
Replace the skylight to starboard of the mast.
Repair the teak skylight on the lower coachroof (rotten timber under the port butterfly wing).
Strap the batteries into battery trays.
Wire the bilge pumps and other 12v connections permanently, and fit an isolation switch for the 12v supply.
Replace the automotive battery chargers with a proper marine charger, with permanent connections in place of crocodile clips.
Fit a gland where the second shore power cable passes through the coaming, if it is to remain in use.
Make good the bare wires at the junction box in the locker by the oven.
Supplement the fire extinguishers to a combined capacity of at least 21A/144B, wall-mounted and positioned throughout the vessel.
Advice
Consider overplating other small areas of the bottom not yet overplated (starboard mid-section 5.0–8.0m and bottom to port 4.0–6.0m).
Fill minor gaps in the plate seams with flexible adhesive sealant.
Reduce the engine oil to the correct level on the dipstick.
Commission a qualified marine engineer to service the engine and keep servicing records.
Consider replacing the primary fuel filter bowl with one rated for 2½ minutes' fire resistance at 600°C (ISO 10088), or relocate the filter outside the engine compartment.
While the vessel remains in the marina, close the fuel shut-off valve at the tank whenever the engine is not in use.
Prepare and paint the coachroofs where rough spots are forming.
Re-tape the peeling seams on the hatch cover by the forward hold.
Extend the coaming around the disused engine room hatch.
Touch up rough spots in the deck coatings.
Identify and mark the unlabelled filler on the port side deck.
Clean up and repaint the corroded angle bar at the break between the side deck and aft deck, to port.
Mouse the anchor shackle (cable ties would suffice).
Check the anchor chain clench.
Monitor the cracked weld on the starboard bitt on the stern deck.
Ensure the companionway hatch is not padlocked from outside while anyone is aboard, so two means of exit are maintained.
Consider commissioning a full gas safety inspection by a qualified gas engineer.
Complete the clipping and support of the freshwater plumbing.
Secure the water tank and calorifier if the vessel's use ever changes from a static, marina-based live-aboard.
Replace the fire extinguishers once more than five years old (current ones dated 2014/2015).
Install fixed ventilation in the accommodation in line with Boat Safety Scheme guidance (not a mandatory requirement).
